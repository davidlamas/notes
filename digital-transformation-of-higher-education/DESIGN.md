# Information architecture and visual encoding

## Visitor journey

The site has two modes over one evidence model.

1. **Presentation** is a 12-step argument: question → scope correction → framework → historical layering → distance precedent → shifts in control → knowledge ecosystem → adoption versus response → cross-level tensions → generative AI → epistemic limits → open hypotheses and signposts.
2. **Explore** lets visitors move between a layered timeline, framework matrices, provisional claims, testable hypotheses, evidence coverage, methods, and bibliography.

The main narrative stays concise. Claim caveats, milestone provenance, paper links, methods, and glossary definitions appear through expandable details and dialogs.

## Public content model

Only the conservative publishable subset is used: paraphrased milestones; synthesis claims; hypotheses; vocabularies and framework; quality flags; gaps; lag examples; sanitized run metadata; glossary entries; and bibliographic facts. The restricted archive, private Consensus links, prompts, abstracts, snippets, and agent traces are excluded.

## Encodings

- **Time:** a piecewise layout gives separate space to pre-1500, 1500–1899, 1900–1999, 2000–2026, and undated material. BCE labels are formatted explicitly.
- **Tracks:** technology, institutions, distance higher education, and knowledge-ecosystem tracks are separate lanes.
- **Date certainty:** stated dates use a solid marker; approximate or inferred dates use a patterned marker; unsourced dates use a visibly cautioned marker; undated items stay in their own column.
- **Continuity/disruption:** continuity, disruption, layering, mixed, and unclear remain separate categories. Text labels accompany colour.
- **Evidence status:** observed/documented, interpretation, debated/speculative, expected, projected, and unspecified are shown as labels rather than a moral colour scale.
- **Valence:** beneficial or harmful readings are always paired with “for whom?” text. No red/green coding is used.
- **Coverage:** region, time, review status, and Pro/Deep counts describe the evidence base; they never rank places or imply stages of development.
- **Cross-highlighting:** selecting a claim or hypothesis filters/highlights linked milestones and exposes the supporting source trail.

## Accessibility and responsive behaviour

All controls are native buttons, links, selects, inputs, details, or dialogs. Presentation controls work with arrow keys. Focus is visible, status changes are announced, motion is suppressed when requested, and colour is never the only carrier of meaning. Timeline items become a readable stacked list on narrow screens while retaining the same filters and detail content.
