# Research gap audit

Audit completed before the October 2026 revision. The unit of analysis is the knowledge claim and its warrant—not the method name or publication venue.

## Collection-level diagnosis

- The six-family taxonomy remains useful and does not need replacing.
- Seven of thirteen featured studies were from CHI. The collection strongly represented design-oriented conceptual work, but weakly represented instrumentation, formal/engineering evaluation, clinical application, child-centred research outside CHI, and non-SIGCHI international HCI.
- Qualitative methods were already shown doing interpretive, critical, participatory, and relational work. Quantitative and technical work had fewer contrasts, making “empirical–analytic” appear narrower than it is.
- The original examples were concentrated between 1999 and 2020 and in North American/European ACM communities.
- Several examples named a tradition convincingly but gave students too little detail about purpose, evidence, contribution, and venue ecology.

## Deliberate contrasts introduced

- ETRA and the lived-informatics study both concern data about people, but one validates a gaze-estimation model while the other interprets tracking as lived practice.
- EICS and research-through-design both build artifacts, but the former warrants an applied competence claim through engineering and evaluation; the latter articulates designerly knowledge through reflective making.
- HRI ethnography and empirical robot evaluation could share a robot and setting while making different kinds of claims.
- IDC, CHI, and INTERACT all host participatory work, showing that a way of knowing is not owned by a conference.
- NordiCHI’s “waves” paper is a conceptual framing paper, not an empirical study; it is included to teach that conference examples can also reshape a field’s vocabulary.

## Remaining limits

The revised collection improves breadth but is not a census. It still underrepresents work published in languages other than English, Global South institutions, Indigenous and decolonial inquiry, accessibility venues outside the selected ecology, formal methods, replication studies, and industry-only research. These omissions are visible in the interface rather than hidden by a completeness claim.
