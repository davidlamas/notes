Academic notes

Maps for thinking.

Questions worth following rarely stay still. These maps evolve as evidence, ideas, and encounters change what can be seen. Wander in, follow a thread, and return from time to time.

Open, evolving work

These are evolving academic inquiries: invitations to inspect, question, use, and extend the ideas presented here, not fixed doctrines or final accounts.

Related, not unified

Different inquiries, independent paths

Each inquiry can be entered and read independently. Connections may emerge, but the collection does not force them into a single theory or method.

Author and curator

About David Lamas

David Lamas is the author, intellectual owner of the original contributions, and responsible curator of these inquiries. He is Professor of Human-Computer Interaction at Tallinn University, where he leads the HCI group and heads the academic direction of Human-Computer Interaction.

His public profiles provide further academic and institutional context. This collection remains a personal academic undertaking rather than an institutional publication.

Citing an inquiry

Certain explorations allow you to freeze the current view by copying its view-specific URL. When referring to the inquiry as a whole, cite its stable public URL. When referring to a particular view, cite the view title and the copied URL. In either case, include David Lamas, the displayed update date, and your access date where appropriate.

Full inquiryLamas, David. “Designerly Ways of Knowing.” Updated 8 November 2026. https://davidlamas.github.io/notes/designerly-ways-of-knowing/ (accessed [date]).

Specific frozen viewLamas, David. “What can we learn by trying to change a situation?” In “Designerly Ways of Knowing.” Updated 8 November 2026. https://davidlamas.github.io/notes/designerly-ways-of-knowing/?v=1&mode=presentation&step=S01&display=reading (accessed [date]).

Revision policy

Card dates mark substantive changes to an inquiry. Minor typographic or technical corrections do not automatically reset them. Stable public URLs are preserved where practical, and the collection landing page records its own revision date.
